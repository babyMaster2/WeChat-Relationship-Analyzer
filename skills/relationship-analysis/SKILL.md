# Relationship Analysis

Analyze only the supplied timeline. Treat automated voice transcripts as fallible and preserve the difference between observed messages and interpretation.

## Privacy and scope

- Keep the analysis local to the active workspace. Do not upload chats, audio, names, or derived profiles to external services.
- Do not infer protected traits, diagnose mental illness or attachment disorders, or present hidden motives as facts.
- If evidence is incomplete, one-sided, heavily redacted, or missing long periods, make that limitation prominent.
- Replace unnecessary real names with “我 / 对方” in the report unless the user asks otherwise.

## Evidence method

For every material conclusion, provide at least one compact evidence item with timestamp, sender, and a short quotation or faithful paraphrase. Prefer multiple independent examples for longitudinal claims.

Label every conclusion as exactly one of:

- **事实**: directly observable in the record, such as message timing, who initiated, or quoted wording.
- **高可信推断**: strongly supported by repeated behavior or converging evidence, while still interpretive.
- **可能解释**: plausible but underdetermined; include at least one alternative explanation.
- **无法判断**: evidence is missing, contradictory, or outside the chat record.

Do not convert message counts, response speed, emojis, gifts, or sexual/romantic language into a numeric “love score.” Quantitative patterns are context, not proof of affection.

## Workflow

1. Validate chronology, participant identity, gaps, duplicated messages, and transcript failure flags.
2. Segment the relationship into evidence-based stages. Use behavior changes rather than arbitrary calendar intervals.
3. Compare both parties over time across:
   - initiation and re-initiation after silence;
   - conversational effort, follow-up questions, and sustained topics;
   - self-disclosure and sharing desire;
   - practical, emotional, and time investment;
   - intimacy, flirtation, boundary testing, and explicit commitments;
   - conflict triggers, escalation, withdrawal, repair attempts, and whether repair lasted;
   - pursue–withdraw cycles without assigning a clinical attachment label;
   - warming, cooling, turning points, and plausible competing explanations.
4. Separate stable patterns from isolated incidents. Note changes in baseline and exceptions.
5. Produce a concise report with an evidence ledger and uncertainty section.

## Report structure

Use these sections when the data supports them:

1. **范围与数据质量** — dates, counts, missing media, transcript reliability, and major gaps.
2. **关系阶段时间线** — each stage with start/end, defining evidence, and confidence.
3. **双方主动性与投入变化** — compare behavior by stage rather than only overall totals.
4. **分享欲、亲密与暧昧** — distinguish friendly warmth, situational intimacy, and explicit romantic signals.
5. **冲突、修复与追逐—回避循环** — trigger → response → repair → outcome.
6. **关键转折点** — rank only well-supported turning points and cite before/after evidence.
7. **对方态度变化** — state what changed in observable communication; do not claim private intent.
8. **我的关系行为模式** — actionable patterns grounded in messages, including counterexamples.
9. **证据台账** — claim, label, timestamp, sender, excerpt/paraphrase, and why it supports the claim.
10. **无法判断与其他解释** — explicitly list the limits of the record.

End with a short, non-prescriptive summary. If the user asks for advice, tie each suggestion to an observed pattern and distinguish low-risk communication experiments from major relationship decisions.
